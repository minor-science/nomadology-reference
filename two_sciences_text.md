Working out the thrust of the stone on site rather than from an architect's plan on paper, the Gothic journeymen who raised the cathedrals practiced what Deleuze and Guattari call a minor or nomad science (364-68). Such a science follows its material and poses problems that it cannot solve on its own (how to vault a nave higher than the Romanesque, for instance). As a result, its findings tend to be taken over by the royal science of the State, which reproduces, deduces, and extracts constants. But the two are not simply opposed, as they share "a single field of interaction" (367) in which what one appropriates, the other is forever cutting loose again. And it is this borderline that the middle column records, whether the passage is by translation, appropriation, repression, or the cutting loose of what was taken. Altogether, the sheet serves as a companion to *The five machinic processes*, which gives the whole matter a single row ("How is knowledge made?").

A nomadology can thus work with anthropology, archaeology, and history without taking on their frame along with their findings. Many of the questions these fields ask, such as how the State originated or who lived free of it, are asked from the side of the State, and each row below turns one of them into a question that a nomadology can pursue, with the pages that ground it and a case from the specialist literature.

The word "nomadology" has more roots than the nomads. In Greek, the pasture came before both the law and any people, and Deleuze took his nomos from that philology in 1968, long before the steppe. In *A Thousand Plateaus* the word first names a lack, and later what monadology turns into once its units open. Each row gives a source and what the word named there, along with what it brings to the name.

Several approaches outside Deleuze and Guattari's orbit look like nomadology from a distance, and each shares something with it. They are useful as contrasts, since what separates them from nomadology is usually where they start, from a people to be given back its history or from a history to be told otherwise.

Our own observation, which Deleuze and Guattari do not make, is that "nomado-*logy*" puts a *logos* over the nomos. Plateau 12 calls this move "the triumph of the logos or the law over the nomos" (373).

*Nomós*, the pasture: "un espace illimité" ("an unlimited space"), a forest, river meadows, a mountainside, and for humans "a habitat without recognized limits"

A distribution of herds across a space no one divides. Only from the fifth century B.C. was the word brought close to *némō*, "to share" (115)

*Nómos*, in which the musical, religious, moral and political senses "appear at once"

Music is as old as law in the word. Law, and "scientific law" (nomology), is the later, narrowed sense (196)

*Nomeús*, the distributor, becomes a title of the king, the "shepherd of peoples," and law is what grazes the human herd

Philosophers turned a word for pasture into a word for rule. A pasture tax belongs to the same family (118)

*Nomádes*, said of the Libyans, is "an adjective" that names "a way of life, not a nation"

The name of a people is the later form. The older one names a way of grazing, dwelling and spreading out

Nomos as "the initial measure and division of pastureland," with "to take" (*nehmen*) as the first meaning of *nemein*

Schmitt cites Laroche and sets the philology aside (324 n. 1), so taking comes first by a jurist's choice, not by etymology

A "nomadic distribution," a nomos "without property, enclosure or measure," taken from Laroche

The word enters Deleuze's philosophy as a way of distributing being, twelve years before any steppe peoples appear in it

Nomadism as "a perpetual displacement in the intensities," and a nomad who "is not necessarily one who moves"

A thought set against philosophy, which "is born out of the imperial state" and stays tied to law (148)

"What is lacking is a Nomadology, the opposite of a history," because history is written "from the sedentary point of view"

The quarrel over the word is mostly a quarrel over the peoples it seems to name

Against origins and against history told from above. "Effective history" places us "among countless lost events, without a landmark or a point of reference" (155)

Genealogy remains a kind of history. Nomadology is "the opposite of a history" (23) and studies processes that coexist in every formation (437)

Refuses the "primitive contemporaries" (25) who were said to have no history. Wolf's world is no "global pool hall" of bounded societies (6)

Wolf puts "the people to whom history has been denied" into "the same historical trajectory" (23). Nomadology doesn't seek a place in history for them. It questions the sedentary point of view from which history is written

Power enters the record at every stage, from making sources and archives to making narratives

Trouillot asks what a record silences. Nomadology also asks what the State converted and then presented as its own invention

Counting as a way of making a population legible, and peoples who live beyond the State's reach, from Zomia to "The Golden Age of the Barbarians"

Scott's units are States and the peoples who escape them. Nomadology names no people. Its processes run inside States as well as outside them

Against stages, they find seasonal changes of social form and societies that chose against hierarchy

Theirs is still an account of what past peoples chose. Nomadology asks how a formation wards off a threshold it has never crossed (437-39)

The steppe itself. Lindner finds the tribe was "a political organism open to all who were willing to subordinate themselves to its chief" (701)

Takes movement seriously, against theories of society built on settlement

The nomad is "he who does not move" (381). What nomadology studies is a distribution in space, not travel

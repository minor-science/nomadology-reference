The word "nomadology" has more roots than the nomads. In Greek, the pasture came before both the law and any people, and Deleuze took his nomos from that philology in 1968, long before the steppe. Even the pasture has a prehistory, since the Bronze Age palaces counted and sorted flocks before Homer's open grazing. In *A Thousand Plateaus* the word first names a lack, and later what monadology turns into once its units open. Each row gives a source and what the word named there, along with what it brings to the name.

Several approaches outside Deleuze and Guattari's orbit come close to nomadology. Rather than grade them, each row ends on the question that would turn its concept toward a nomadology. The letters point to the roots on the front page, and the numbers to the guiding problems on the back of the sheet on royal and nomad science.

Our own observation, which Deleuze and Guattari do not make, is that "nomado-*logy*" puts a *logos* over the nomos. Plateau 12 calls this move "the triumph of the logos or the law over the nomos" (373).

Flocks the palaces counted by place, shepherd, sign and number, often under a "collector," with rams castrated for "more homogeneous fleeces" (122)

The undivided pasture is no origin before the State. In Zartaloudis's account it follows the palaces' administered flocks, once their collapse freed land for grazing (125). The collector's word, *a-ko-ra*, a gathering of flocks, later names the assembly, the agora (129-30)

*Nomós*, the pasture: "un espace illimité" ("an unlimited space"), a forest, river meadows, a mountainside, and for humans "a habitat without recognized limits"

A distribution of herds across a space no one divides. Only from the fifth century B.C. was the word brought close to *némō*, "to share" (115). The two words, *nomós* and *nómos*, were later told apart by accent (Zartaloudis (xxvii))

*Nómos*, in which the musical, religious, moral and political senses "appear at once"

Music is as old as law in the word, and laws were sung before they were written. The early lawgivers called theirs *thesmoi*, not *nómoi* (Zartaloudis (387-90)). Law, and "scientific law" (nomology), is the later, narrowed sense (196)

*Nomeús*, the distributor, becomes a title of the king, the "shepherd of peoples," and law is what grazes the human herd

Philosophers turned a word for pasture into a word for rule. A pasture tax belongs to the same family (118). In Homer, "shepherd of the people" is not yet a political title (Zartaloudis (139))

*Nomádes*, said of the Libyans, is "an adjective" that names "a way of life, not a nation"

The name of a people is the later form. The older one names a way of grazing, dwelling and spreading out

Nomos as "the initial measure and division of pastureland," with "to take" (*nehmen*) as the first meaning of *nemein*

Schmitt cites Laroche and sets the philology aside (324 n. 1), so taking comes first by a jurist's choice, not by etymology. His nomos also rests on a contested Homeric reading, *nómon* for *noon* at *Odyssey* 1.3, which is carved on his tombstone (Zartaloudis (117-18))

A "nomadic distribution," a nomos "without property, enclosure or measure," taken from Laroche

The word enters Deleuze's philosophy as a way of distributing being, twelve years before any steppe peoples appear in it. The word can only be *nomós*, the pasture, though Deleuze never marks the accent (Zartaloudis (141))

Nomadism as "a perpetual displacement in the intensities," and a nomad who "is not necessarily one who moves"

A thought set against philosophy, which "is born out of the imperial state" and stays tied to law (148)

"What is lacking is a Nomadology, the opposite of a history," because history is written "from the sedentary point of view"

The word first names something missing

The name is one alias among others, and a joke: "no one clearly understood" the discipline's goals or method

Miller traces the nomads to the colonial anthropology in the notes. Braidotti makes the nomad a figure of the subject. Bogue defends a "bastard concept." Zartaloudis finds the pairing of nomad and settled idealizing and historically imprecise (142 n. 48)

The quarrel over the word is mostly a quarrel over the peoples it seems to name

A power exercised "not over a territory" but over "the flock in its movement" (125, cited in Zartaloudis 139), caring for each one and for all together. It starts where nomadology starts, from a multiplicity on the move

When a power tends a flock on the move, each one and all together, is the flock composed by its own movement or by the shepherd's count?

Nomos as a field of coexisting uses that jurists later projected back as law. A nomos, following Hirzel, is "a power that is; not a power that is 'had'" (xxxvii)

If nomos was first a way of life, what had to be converted before it could be presented as a law that was always there?

The same word, and the question of how the earth is ordered, with taking first, dividing second and pasture last
